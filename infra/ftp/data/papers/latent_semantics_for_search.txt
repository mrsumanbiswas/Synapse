From Latent Semantic Analysis to Contextual Embeddings: Matching Concepts Instead of Words
Authors: Mei Lin and Tomas Varga
Contact: mei.lin@synapse-demo.example.org
Published: 2025
Note: sample document written for the Synapse demo corpus; the references are real.

Abstract
Keyword search fails when a user asks for "car" and the relevant paper only says "automobile" or "vehicle". This
note traces how retrieval moved from matching words to matching concepts: latent semantic analysis factorises the
term-document matrix with a truncated singular value decomposition, probabilistic topic models give the factors a
generative story, and neural embeddings learn dense vectors directly from text.

Keywords: latent semantic analysis, singular value decomposition, topic models, word embeddings

1 Latent semantic analysis
Latent semantic indexing applies a truncated SVD to the TF-IDF weighted term-document matrix and keeps around
one hundred factors [1]. Queries are folded into the same space, so documents can match without sharing a single
term with the query. The same mathematics was later argued to explain how people acquire word meaning from
co-occurrence (Landauer and Dumais, 1997), which we cite as [2].

2 Probabilistic topic models
Probabilistic latent semantic indexing recasts the factors as latent topics with a proper likelihood [3], and
latent Dirichlet allocation adds priors so that unseen documents receive topic mixtures [4]. Both remain popular
for exploring large collections [3, 4].

3 Neural embeddings
Word2vec learns word vectors by predicting context words and made semantic similarity cheap to compute [5];
GloVe factorises global co-occurrence counts instead [6]. Attention-based transformers [7] and their pre-trained
encoders [8] produce contextual vectors that now power semantic search (arXiv:1706.03762). The lineage from
SVD [1] to embeddings [5-8] is direct: all of them compress co-occurrence statistics.
